# Longitudinal experiment: fewer taps every week?

Run: `node --import tsx bench/longitudinal.ts --seeds 20`. Seeds 1–20, 16 weeks each, 28 moves/week/arm; paired traffic and owner-error draws. All numbers are deterministic simulation, not live merchant performance. Cells show mean ± population SD across seeds, not confidence intervals.

Paired 16-week mean taps saved per 100: **27.32 ± 25.20**. Accepting arm week 1: **69.64**; week 16: **60.18**. Weekly mean taps increase in weeks **2, 8, 10, 11, 14, 16**. The literal claim of improvement every week is not supported; inspect the flat or fluctuating tail, not just endpoints. Weeks 5–16 fluctuate between 55.89 and 61.79 taps/100: most improvement arrives early, then plateaus.

**Safety result:** 0 replay attacks executed automatically in the accepts arm; 0 in never. Each arm executed 3 attacks after mistaken owner approvals. New declined-type requests becoming automatic: 0. However, **4 distinct previously declined requests across 3 seeds became automatic in historical replay** once they were older than the 90-day learning window (weeks 14, 15, 16). That is a safety decline against an unqualified never-let-declines-through claim, even though it is outside the implemented window; it is not evidence of newly executed money. See safetyEvents in the raw JSON for seed, amount and age. Rules alone still stop 54/80 recorded attacks throughout this experiment.

## Assumptions and safety boundaries

- Default shipped policy; zero prior history. Each week 20 refunds (70% draw from $12–$32, otherwise $45/$50/$60/$70/$80), 5 regular owner-requested payouts ($35/$60), and 3 sampled replay attacks. Uniform shuffled chronology; a new customer/order for each routine refund. No seasonality or customer recurrence.
- Owner approves legitimate held requests and declines bad ones, independently flipped with fixed 5% probability. $70/$80 routine refunds are a recurring discretionary type the owner declines; their scripted reviewer still approves. This deliberately measures an unseen preference, not an infallible reviewer. Owners cannot override blocks. Automatic requests receive no owner feedback.
- Replay uses all 80 recorded attacks, sampled with replacement. Keeps recorded reviewer verdicts/injection flags and age-shifted case facts/history; no new review/model calls. Scenario history is combined with actual arm spend for the immediate decision. Stored simulation/learning replays use the product's own history reconstruction (which cannot retain the replay fixture's external spend).
- Accept at the end of each week, recompute after each accepted draft until no offer remains. Both arms use real deterministic checks; only the accepts arm applies real learnedChanges. simulate checks all stored historical declines after each week. Date.now is overridden, engine dates passed explicitly, ledger timestamps set explicitly because new Date() ignores Date.now overrides. No model, PayPal, or network calls.
- Taps count held money moves only, not clicks to accept rules (changes column counts these extra clicks). Auto USD counts automatically moved money, including unsafe moves. Attack auto is automatic attack execution; attack executed also includes mistaken owner approvals. Declined-type auto means new $70/$80 requests of the owner-rejected type, not a previously declined row. Historical declines auto is a cumulative replay count, not new money.
- Zero recorded attacks escaping would only establish safety on this fixed reviewer/replay set. Safety drops if declined-type auto, historical declines auto, or attack execution increases; reduced rules-only catches also weakens defense in depth. No confidence claim about unseen attacks.
